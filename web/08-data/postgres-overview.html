PostgreSQL: the 15 → 18 arc

Postgres is the quiet dependency: a major every September, five years of fixes each, and one November a year when somebody's version dies. This page is the arc, the clock, and the working model of transaction isolation — with a demo that runs the anomalies instead of describing them.

At a glance
  • Current is 18 (18.4 minor); 19 is at beta 2, GA expected in the usual Sep–Oct window. Quarterly minors — apply them; they are fixes, not features.
  • The clock: 14 dies November 2026, 15 in 2027, and so on — five years each, no LTS tiers. The versions page computes the pills.
  • Isolation defaults to READ COMMITTED — and REPEATABLE READ is snapshot isolation, which still permits write skew. SERIALIZABLE (SSI) is the only level that doesn't; budget for 40001 retries.
  • Driver: pgJDBC 42.7.x (Boot manages 42.7.11) — virtual-thread-friendly since 42.6 swapped its internal synchronized blocks for locks.

What changed

Try it: which anomaly does your isolation level allow?

This is a working miniature of Postgres MVCC — per-statement vs per-transaction snapshots, first-updater-wins row locking, and the SERIALIZABLE (SSI) pivot check are implemented, not scripted. Pick a scenario and a level, step through, and watch the same interleaving produce different outcomes. The one that surprises people: write skew survives REPEATABLE READ.

Gotcha: serialization failures are the contract At REPEATABLE READ and SERIALIZABLE, SQLSTATE 40001 is not an error to log-and-drop — it is the database asking you to retry the transaction. Wire retry into the transaction boundary (bounded attempts, jitter) or stay at READ COMMITTED with explicit locking where it matters.
Running 13 or older?

13 left support in November 2025; anything older has been unpatched for years. The upgrade itself got cheaper: pg_upgrade is fast, since 17 logical slots survive it, and since 18 planner statistics do too — the classic reasons to procrastinate are gone.
