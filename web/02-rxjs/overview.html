RxJS in 2026

Signals took over the job RxJS was doing badly. What is left is the job it does better than anything else — and there is a lot of it.

At a glance
  • 7.8.2 is current and will be for a while. RxJS 8 has been in alpha since 2025 and is deliberately on hold until Observable is standardised on the web platform. Do not plan around a date.
  • Signals won synchronous state. If a value is just "the current thing", it is a signal now.
  • RxJS keeps time. Debouncing, cancellation, retries, sequencing, websockets, combining several async sources — none of which signals attempt.
  • The two interoperate: toSignal() and toObservable().

Is RxJS still worth learning?

Yes, but a smaller amount of it than in 2020. The realistic working set for an Angular application in 2026 is about fifteen operators — and the ones that matter are precisely the ones signals cannot replace, because they are about time rather than value.

What has genuinely gone away is RxJS as a state-management strategy. The BehaviorSubject-per-piece-of-state pattern, the combineLatest of five subjects to compute a boolean, the shareReplay(1) sprinkled to stop duplicate work — all of that was RxJS being pressed into a role it was not designed for, and signals do it with no ceremony at all.

The mental model

An Observable is a function that, when subscribed to, starts producing values and eventually completes or errors. Four properties follow from that, and most confusion traces back to one of them:

PropertyConsequence
LazyNothing happens until you subscribe. An HTTP Observable you never subscribe to makes no request.
Multi-valueUnlike a Promise it can emit many times — that is the whole point.
CancellableUnsubscribing genuinely aborts the work. A Promise cannot do this.
Cold by defaultEach subscriber gets its own run. Two subscribers means two HTTP requests.
Cold vs. hot is the source of most surprises A cold Observable starts its work per subscriber. http.get() is cold, which is why subscribing twice fires two requests, and why the same Observable in a template three times fires three. A hot Observable produces independently and shares one run — a Subject, a websocket, a DOM event stream. share() and shareReplay() turn cold into hot.

What changed

If you meet a codebase doing obs.map(…).filter(…) with imports from rxjs/add/operator/…, it predates 2018 and has not been touched since. That style does not work in RxJS 7 at all.

Where RxJS 8 is

Parked, on purpose. The maintainers are waiting for the TC39/WHATWG Observable proposal so that RxJS 8 can align with a platform primitive rather than diverge from one. The most recent alpha is from January 2025. This is a deliberate decision rather than a stalled project, and the practical consequence is simply that 7.8 is stable ground — code written against it is not about to be invalidated.

What that does mean: operators already marked for removal in v8 or v9 (toPromise, retryWhen, flatMap, mapTo, switchMapTo, the publish* family) are worth migrating away from now, since the replacements all exist in 7.8 and are better anyway.
