Lab: write your own sorts

Reading unit 3 is not the same as owning it. Implement a sort; the harness checks correctness on friendly and adversarial inputs, then instruments your code with counting proxies and reports the complexity it actually exhibited — the course's "counters are the curriculum" promise, turned on you.

Before you start Merge sort or quicksort — implement whichever you like, or an elementary sort first to watch the harness call out its n².
Why the adversarial cases

Already-sorted, reverse-sorted, and all-equal inputs are where homemade sorts historically die (naive quicksort's n² pivot, off-by-one at boundaries, dropped duplicates). They are also exactly what production data looks like — mostly-sorted logs, constant columns. The multiset check catches the classic bug family where output is sorted but wrong: elements dropped, duplicated, or invented.
